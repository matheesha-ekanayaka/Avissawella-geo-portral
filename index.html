About the village

Where the lowlands end and the hills begin.

Sitting at the edge of Sri Lanka's wet zone, Avissawella has always been a threshold place — once a defended royal capital, later a colonial checkpoint, and today the last significant town before the A4 climbs toward Ratnapura and the highlands beyond. Rubber, tea, and now light industry move through it; the Kelani river and its tributaries wind around it; and the ruins of a kingdom that outlasted three Portuguese governors still sit quietly in the surrounding jungle.

This site exists to make that layered identity easy to find — its history, its landmarks, and its geography — in one place, for residents, students, and visitors alike.

01 · Heritage

Seven decades that shaped a nation's resistance

When the Kotte kingdom split in the early 16th century, Seetawaka became the inland stronghold from which two rulers, father and son, held off Portuguese expansion for longer than any other Sinhalese kingdom of the period.

1521
1521

Founding of Seetawaka

The Kotte kingdom fractures into three parts. Mayadunne establishes his capital at Seetawaka — modern Avissawella — choosing an inland, defensible site away from the coastal ports the Portuguese already controlled.

1550s–70s
1550s–70s

Decades of resistance

Mayadunne repeatedly repels Portuguese incursions launched from Colombo, turning Seetawaka into the most persistent center of opposition to colonial expansion on the island.

1581
1581

Rajasinha I takes the throne

Mayadunne's son inherits the kingdom and pushes it to its greatest territorial extent, at one point laying siege to the Portuguese fort at Colombo itself.

1593
1593

The kingdom fragments

Following Rajasinha I's death, Seetawaka's territory is absorbed by Kandy and the coastal powers. Independent rule ends, but the name — and the ruins scattered through the surrounding forest — remain.

Today
Today

A modern junction town

Avissawella now anchors the Seethawaka Industrial Zone, a working railway terminus, and a market that serves as the last urban stop before the road climbs toward Ratnapura and the central highlands.

02 · Nature & Landmarks

Five places that define the village today

From forest waterfalls to a colonial-era railway terminus — the landmarks below are also mapped, with coordinates, on the Geo-Portal.

Sacred

Aranya Senasanaya Temple

A forest-monastery temple known for intricate carving and a quiet, garden-set atmosphere — a working center of Sinhalese Buddhist practice in the heart of town.

Waterfall

Kumari Ella (Kumari Falls)

A forest waterfall in the hills outside town, reached on foot through rubber and tea smallholdings — a popular cool-season hike for locals and visitors.

Conservation

Seethawaka Wet Zone Botanic Garden

A rainforest conservation garden protecting native lowland wet-zone flora — one of the newer botanic gardens developed to preserve Sri Lanka's rapidly shrinking rainforest habitat.

Viewpoint

Ranmuthugala Hill

A short hike from the town center leads to sweeping views over surrounding forest and hills — a favourite spot for a sunrise or sunset walk.

Transport

Avissawella Railway Station

The historic terminus of the Kelani Valley line, once built to move tea, rubber, and graphite to Colombo — still an active station and a quiet piece of colonial-era infrastructure.

Industry

Seethawaka Industrial Zone

A modern export-processing and light-industrial zone on the edge of town, reflecting Avissawella's ongoing transition from royal capital to regional economic hub.
